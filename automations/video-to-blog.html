Video to Blog

One Slack command turns a YouTube video into a full blog draft in Notion.

Read the runbook
01 · The request

One command, one link.

Type /video-to-blog and a YouTube link in the team's Slack channel. The workflow answers straight away that it's working on it, and takes it from there.

02 · The soundtrack

The video's words, read off the reel.

Supadata fetches the full transcript of the video. The workflow gives it thirty seconds to settle, then reads every word.

transcript
03 · The cut

Fillers out, paragraphs in.

The ums, uhs and you-knows are cut out. What's left is split every five sentences, so the writer gets clean paragraphs instead of one long breath.

04 · The draft

A full article, in the house voice.

Claude reads the transcript end to end and writes the post: the SEO details first, then a TL;DR, a hook, the sections, an "In a nutshell" and the FAQs. Around 6,500 to 8,500 words.

05 · Into Notion

Filed as a draft, never published.

A Notion page is created with the slug, meta description and primary keyword, and its status set to draft. A long post goes in up to three batches, because Notion takes a hundred blocks at a time.

06 · The ping

The channel hears it's ready.

Slack gets the title, slug, keyword, word count, the number of FAQs and a link to review the draft in Notion.

07 · One reel at a time

Wait for the link before the next one.

Run one conversion at a time; back-to-back links can fail or come out incomplete. And a video needs captions: one without them, or blocked in a region, has nothing to read.

no captions, no draft

A YouTube link in. A full blog draft in Notion.

One command, one reel at a time. Every draft waits for a person to review it.

01Problem

A good video already holds a whole article.

Getting it out meant transcribing the talk, cleaning it up, outlining it and writing it again as a post, by hand, before an editor could even look at it.

02Running it

In the team's Slack channel, type the command with the video's link and press Enter.

/video-to-blog https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=…

Slack answers at once that the post is being worked on. When it's done, a message arrives in the same channel:

📄 New blog draft ready for review Title How to read a pipeline report without guessing Slug /blog/read-a-pipeline-report Primary keyword pipeline report Word count 7,420 FAQs 8 questions Review in Notion ↗

An illustrative example. The real message carries the draft's own details and its Notion link.

05Guardrails

  • One at a timeDon't send links back to back. Wait for the Notion link to appear in the channel before starting the next one.
  • Captions firstThe video needs a transcript. Videos without captions, or blocked in some regions, have nothing to convert.
  • Always a draftThe page is filed with its status set to draft. Nothing is published without a person reviewing it.
  • Long posts arrive wholeThe body is sent to Notion in batches of up to a hundred blocks, so a long post isn't cut off.

06Outcome

1Slack command per video, nothing else to fill in
6.5–8.5kwords the draft is written to
100blocks per batch into Notion, so long posts arrive whole

A recorded talk becomes a structured, SEO-ready draft in Notion without anyone transcribing, outlining or retyping it. The editor's time goes into reviewing, not into the first pass.

07Operating it

  • Knowing it's doneThe summary message with the Notion link arrives in the channel. Until then, the conversion is still running.
  • Something failedOpen the Executions tab in n8n to see which step stopped and why.
  • The usual causeA video with no transcript available. Check the video has captions before sending the command again.
