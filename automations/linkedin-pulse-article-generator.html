LinkedIn Pulse Article Generator

Mark a topic cluster "yes" in a sheet. Its old posts are recast into one new pillar article, waiting in Notion.

01 · The order book

A row says yes.

A sheet holds the topic clusters: a topic, its pillar keyword, search intent, the cluster's role, a category and up to four existing posts. Set a row's generate column to "yes". The sheet is checked every minute.

yes
02 · Old bells in

Four posts, fetched by slug.

Every post in the Webflow blog collection is read once. The row's four slugs are matched to their bodies, which are stripped down to plain text.

03 · The pattern

The posts are the brief.

There's no separate SEO brief: the four posts are the brief. Write one new, original article, take the H2s from their themes, and add an SEO pack and seven to nine FAQs.

04 · Recast

Melted down. Nothing copied.

Claude writes one pillar of 3,000 to 4,000 words in a marketing leader's voice: a TL;DR first, a scene for a hook, sentence-case H2s, "In a nutshell", the FAQs, and a meta-data table on top.

no sentence copied
05 · Tuned

Checked against the voice.

Contractions, no em dashes, no sentence over 30 words, no one-line paragraphs, the banned phrases out, six to eight italics and one "Mic drop."

06 · Into Notion

Built block by block.

A draft page with the topic, cluster, primary keyword, category, search intent, source count and status "Generated". The article becomes Notion blocks (headings, lists, real tables, the meta-data pinned on top), added 100 at a time.

07 · Marked done

The row says done.

The sheet row flips to "done", and the loop moves on to the next "yes".

donenext yes

Four posts in. One pillar out.

A row marked "yes" becomes a pillar draft of 3,000 to 4,000 words in Notion, written from posts you already have, with nothing copied.

01Problem

Four good posts, and no page that ties them together.

A blog collects posts on the same topic over the years. Each one is fine on its own, but together they compete with each other, and nobody has written the one long page that pulls them into a whole. Writing that pillar by hand means rereading all of them first, then making sure nothing is lifted from them.

02Running it

  1. 01

    Fill a row

    One row per cluster: a topic, the primary keyword, search intent, the cluster's role, a category, and the titles and slugs of up to four existing posts.

  2. 02

    Say yes

    Set the row's generate column to "yes". The sheet is checked every minute.

  3. 03

    Let it write

    The posts are fetched, the pillar is written, and the page is built in Notion. This takes a few minutes.

  4. 04

    Edit in Notion

    Open the new draft page (status "Generated"), read it and edit it like any other draft.

  5. 05

    Next

    The row now says "done". Any other "yes" rows run in turn.

04The article

05If something fails

  • Start hereThe Executions tab of the workflow in n8n shows which step stopped and why.
  • A row stays "yes"The run stopped before the row was marked done. The usual causes: a slug that matched no post, or the page build in Notion failing partway.
  • A post is missing from the articleIts slug in the sheet doesn't match the slug in Webflow exactly. Copy it from Webflow.
  • The same cluster appears twice in NotionA long run was still going when the next check picked the row up again. Keep one page and delete the other.
  • A long post is only partly usedEach post is trimmed to its first 9,000 characters before Claude reads it.

06Outcome

~1 minuntil a row marked "yes" is picked up
3–4kwords in every pillar draft
7–9FAQs, numbered, in every article

Clusters of old posts become pillar drafts without anyone rereading them first. The time goes into editing one strong page, not into writing it from scratch.

07Operating it

  • Only "yes" runsRows marked "done" or left blank are never touched.
  • Slugs must matchThe workflow finds each post by its Webflow slug, so copy the slug rather than typing it.
  • It's a draftPages land with status "Generated". An editor reads, edits and publishes.
  • One row at a timeSeveral "yes" rows queue up and run one after another.
